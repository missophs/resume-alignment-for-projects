# Humanize Protocol (mandatory on every delivery, all modes)

## Why this exists
Two detection signals matter most: perplexity (how predictable the word choices are — low perplexity reads as AI) and burstiness (how much sentence length varies — humans write in bursts, AI is uniform). Secondary signals: overused transitions ("furthermore," "moreover," "in conclusion"), clustered hedge words, symmetric "not only X but also Y" structures, noun-heavy phrasing over verb-driven action, paragraph conclusions that restate the paragraph, uniform formality, absence of concrete specifics, and suspiciously perfect logical flow with no pivots. The fix: inject unpredictability at every level — word choice, sentence length, structure, register, rhythm.

## Pass 1 — Structural check
Cut any bullet's trailing recap sentence. Rebuild any section with a too-predictable parallel structure. Identify the underlying argument and ignore the AI's original structure entirely — consider starting mid-thought, moving the conclusion to an unexpected position, or breaking one long logical chain into two shorter ones with a gap between them.

## Pass 2 — Sentence-level surgery
For every sentence, ask: would a human naturally write this exact construction? If not: rewrite in a different syntactic form, change the length (shorten long ones, occasionally lengthen short ones), replace the most predictable word with a natural but less common synonym, convert passive to active, and cut transition words that exist only to connect.

## Pass 3 — Vocabulary replacement
leverage/utilize → use · furthermore/moreover → cut, or start a new paragraph · "in conclusion" → cut · "it is worth noting that" → cut the phrase, keep the note · robust → strong/solid/reliable, or cut · comprehensive → thorough/full/complete · facilitate → help/allow/enable · ensure → make sure · innovative → the specific thing that makes it different · transformative → the specific thing that changes · streamline → simplify/speed up/cut steps from · synergy → the specific way two things work together · ecosystem → industry/space/network, or cut · underscore → shows/confirms/points to · orchestrated, championed, pioneered, helmed → banned entirely, no substitute needed, remove the claim's frame instead.

## Pass 4 — Burstiness injection
**This does not override the XYZ formula — a bullet's X, Y, and Z must each be fully flushed out first (see `bullet-formula-and-verbs.md`); burstiness is achieved by varying how much a fully-formed bullet elaborates, never by cutting a bullet down to a fragment.** Resume bullets carrying a real accomplishment, an exact metric, and a named mechanism don't compress below roughly 15-18 words — don't force one shorter than that just to hit a word-count target. Audit bullet lengths within each role: if three consecutive bullets land within 5 words of each other, break the pattern by letting one bullet elaborate more (a longer clause after the semicolon, an added piece of context) or land closer to the floor (a tighter but still complete X, Y, Z) — vary elaboration, not completeness. Never more than two consecutive bullets at the same approximate length. A short bullet in a role's rotation is still a full bullet with all three parts present, just less elaborated than its neighbors — it is never a fragment.

## Pass 5 — Perplexity check + human imperfection
Substitute less predictable (but still accurate) synonyms wherever a word choice reads as the statistically obvious pick. In cover letters and summaries only (never bullets): add one informal contraction ("it's," "that's," "you'll"), let one sentence end more casually than the surrounding text, and include one direct, unhedged assertion that reads like an opinion.

## Pass 6 — Opening and closing check
Summary S1 opens with the specific thing Melissa did and what it produced — never "In today's [adjective] world," never a rhetorical question, never a definition ("X is defined as..."), never an adjective-led opener ("Seasoned," "Strategic," "Results-driven"). Start with the accomplishment itself, not the setup to it, or start mid-idea. The last bullet of each role ends with weight — a number or a business-consequence noun, never a summary of what was just said, never an inspirational close, never a symmetrical mirror of the opening.

## Humanize quality checklist — run before every delivery
- Opening avoids hollow setup, question, or definition
- No 3+ consecutive bullets within 5 words of each other in length
- All high-frequency AI vocabulary replaced
- Every bullet's X, Y, and Z are each fully flushed out — none compressed into a fragment to create a short bullet (a bullet under ~15 words is almost always a sign X, Y, or Z got cut short, not a stylistic choice)
- Closing bullet of each role ends on a number or consequence noun
- No transition words used purely as connectors
- At least one concrete specific per role (metric, named tool, timeframe, named org)
- No passive voice unless deliberate
- No banned words (see `facts-and-banned-words.md`)

## Melissa's voice profile
Short to medium sentences, punchy, direct, rarely complex. Contractions used freely in cover letters, never in bullets. Formality: 6/10 — professional but not stiff. Strong declarative statements, minimal hedging, minimal connective transitions — let paragraphs stand on their own. Signature move: a direct assertion followed by specific evidence. Signature opener: start with the point, not the context. HR boilerplate, motivational language, and corporate-speak are out regardless of context. For the specific banned words, use the single list in `facts-and-banned-words.md` — don't keep a second copy here, not even a partial example list. That's exactly the bug that let a banned word silently drift back in through this file once already; even an illustrative excerpt goes stale the next time Melissa adds a word.
